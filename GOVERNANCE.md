# Governance

Origo starts as a **benevolent maintainer** project (a small team led by its founder) and will grow toward a **technical committee** as contributors emerge. The goal is always: technical merit, transparency, and serving users.

## Principles

- **Open** — decisions happen in public issues, PRs and discussions wherever possible.
- **Merit** — influence is earned through sustained, high-quality contribution, not title, degree, or employer.
- **Rough consensus** — we seek agreement; when consensus fails, maintainers decide and record the reason.
- **Upstream first** — prefer fixing problems in upstream projects and feeding patches back.
- **Stability** — once an interface is public, breaking changes need a deprecation path.

## Roles

- **Founder / Lead Maintainer** — sets the vision, architecture, and release cadence; final decision authority.
- **Maintainers** — merge rights in a subsystem (agent, skills, iso, docs). Appointed after consistent, trusted contributions.
- **Contributors** — anyone who submits a merged change (DCO signed).
- **Users** — feedback and issues drive the roadmap.

## Becoming a maintainer

A sustained record (typically multiple merged PRs, reliability, and constructive collaboration) plus nomination by an existing maintainer. The founder grants/removes merge rights; removals are recorded with a reason.

## Technical Committee (future)

When the project has 5+ active maintainers, day-to-day decisions move to a technical committee with a documented voting process. Major changes (license, trademarks, governance, breaking releases) require a supermajority.

## Assets and licensing

- Code: Apache-2.0 (project code) plus upstream licenses; see [LICENSE](LICENSE).
- Trademarks (Origo / 元启 name and logos) must be held by a stable legal entity — initially the project, later transferred to a foundation (e.g. an open-source foundation) once one exists.
- Contributions are made under the DCO.
